## Direct answers to common searches

### What is Oakland Measure EE?

**Oakland Measure EE** is a **City of Oakland Charter Amendment** on the **November 3, 2026** ballot (Alameda County lettered the measure on August 10, 2026). Official legislative file: **Legistar 26-0692**. It would make the Mayor Oakland’s chief executive, add a legislative veto and a **line-item budget veto**, require City Council to serve full-time, and let the Public Ethics Commission set elected officials’ salaries. It needs only a **simple majority (50% + 1)** to pass. This site urges voters to **vote NO**.

Do not confuse Measure EE with **Measure FF** (a real-property transfer tax on certain foreclosures). EE is the charter rewrite.

### What is the Oakland Charter Amendment?

The **Oakland Charter Amendment** on the November 2026 ballot is **Measure EE**. If adopted, it rewrites who runs the city for every future mayor—not only the current mayor. Official ballot title style: **Measure EE — City of Oakland Charter Amendment**.

### What is the Barbara Lee ballot measure?

The **Barbara Lee ballot measure** commonly refers to **Measure EE**, the strong-mayor **Oakland Charter Amendment**. When Oakland City Council deadlocked **4–4** on placing it on the ballot, **Mayor Barbara Lee** cast the **tie-breaking vote** for a measure that expands the Mayor’s office’s power. Councilmembers Zac Unger, Janani Ramachandran, Noel Gallo, and Ken Houston voted no.

## Official ballot question

Shall the measure to amend Oakland's City Charter to, among other things, (1) make the Mayor the City's chief executive with a legislative veto, (2) empower the Council to override vetoes, confirm appointments of certain directors, and appoint an Independent Budget and Legislative Analyst, (3) affirm Councilmembers' right to acquire information from other City officials, (4) require Councilmembers to work full-time, and (5) empower the Public Ethics Commission to set salaries for all City elected officials using similar formulas, be adopted?

---

## What Measure EE actually does

1. **Makes the Mayor Oakland’s CEO** — hiring and managing the City Administrator and directing departments, agencies, and appointed offices.
2. **Creates a legislative veto and a line-item budget veto.** Council can override only with six of eight votes (two-thirds).
3. **Limits confirmation** to four directors (Finance, Human Resources, Public Works, Transportation) — not the City Administrator.
4. **Creates a new Independent Budget and Legislative Analyst office** the City Auditor prices at **$1.7–$1.8 million** a year.
5. **Forces Council full-time** with a ban on outside employment, then lets the Public Ethics Commission reset pay every two years starting in 2027.

## Cost, pay pathway, and fiscal context

- **City Auditor impartial analysis (July 8, 2026):** **$2.0–$2.6 million** first-year cost, then inflation.
- **Current council salary:** $114,678 per year.
- **Legal pathway if Measure EE passes:** about **$245,000** (up to **125%**) if salaries are benchmarked to Los Angeles-level full-time officials.
- **Extra council salary and benefits (year one):** $36,000–$108,000 per member; **$288,000–$867,000** citywide.
- Oakland has declared a state of **extreme fiscal necessity** for a fourth straight year. Finance has forecast a **$115–$135 million** annual structural shortfall through 2030. Voters rejected Mayor Lee’s June parcel tax, **Measure E**.

---

## Neighborhood power and the line-item veto

Under Measure EE the Mayor can zero out funding for specific district projects. Overriding a veto requires **6 of 8** council votes, so the Mayor needs only **three allies** to block neighborhood priorities. Councilmember Zac Unger warned that without budget primacy, members become a “punching bag to absorb bad vibes.”

---

## How it reached the ballot

- Council vote to place Measure EE on the ballot deadlocked **4–4**.
- **NO:** Zac Unger, Janani Ramachandran, Noel Gallo, Ken Houston.
- **YES:** Kevin Jenkins, Charlene Wang, Carroll Fife, Rowena Brown.
- **Mayor Barbara Lee** broke the tie.
- A **Council-Manager** alternative was blocked from the ballot.
- Councilmember Carroll Fife: public perception is a “pure power grab.” She also said Oakland does not have “the trust right now to invest in a single leader.”
